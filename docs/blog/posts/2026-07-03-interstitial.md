<div class="poem-text">

Beside the road I make my camp,
While westbound engines hum and roll;
The prairie holds a patch of lamp,
A beacon turning through the whole.

A hundred years before this night,
A rider paused where grasses bend;
He watched the same cold stars alight,
Unsure what waited at the end.

The dirt road grew to lanes of steel,
The horse to truck, the fire to screens;
Yet still the wandering seems real,
Between the places and the means.

The beacon sweeps across the dark,
For other travelers passing through;
It finds me here and leaves its mark,
A fleeting light, then lost from view.
