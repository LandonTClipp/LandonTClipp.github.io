title: The Fire

<div class="poem" markdown>

<div class="poem-text">

I sat beside the dying fire
While mountain peaks held the sky.
The pines leaned close around the hill,
And no one asked me why.

<!-- more -->

The coals were red. The night was still.
The moon rode pale and small.
The darkness gathered at the edge.

The fire made room for all.

A traveler climbed from down the ridge
And settled by the flame.
He carried mud upon his boots
And twine within his frame.

He spoke of days among the woods,
Of careful plans concealed,
Of boxes hidden under stones
And promises still sealed.

His eyes would often drift away
Toward trails beyond the wall.

The fire made room for all.

Another wandered from the dark
And warmed his hands awhile.
The wonder in his weathered face
Had never left his smile.

He told of colors in the sky
That danced the night before,
And deer that stepped through morning mist
Outside his canvas door.

The stars reflected in his eyes
As though they still might fall.

The fire made room for all.

A third emerged between the pines
And watched the forest rim.
At every crack of branch or twig
His gaze returned to them.

He laughed about some nameless fright
That sent him from his bed,
Though now and then he touched a scar
And looked away instead.

The woods seemed larger where he sat,
The shadows strangely tall.

The fire made room for all.

The last man climbed the hill alone
And chose the farthest stone.
The others watched him quietly
And left him to his own.

He spoke of wounds that would not close,
Though years had come and gone.
Of giving all he knew to give
And finding little dawn.

The embers settled as he talked.
No wind disturbed them all.

The fire made room for all.

The moon climbed high above the ridge.
The fire burned low and red.
The strangers sat in silent rows
With little left unsaid.

Then one by one they seemed to fade,
Like smoke upon the air.
The hopeful man grew faint and thin,
Then vanished from his chair.

The dreamer followed soon behind,
Then one who feared the trees.
The lonely man upon the stone
Departed with the breeze.

I sat beside the dying fire
Beneath the mountain wall.

And in the ash remained the prints
Of boots I’d worn through all.
