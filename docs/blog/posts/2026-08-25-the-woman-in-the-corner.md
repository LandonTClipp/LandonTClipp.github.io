title: The Woman in the Corner
---

_Taken from a perspective that is not my own._

<!-- more -->

<div class="poem" markdown>

<div class="poem-text">

I saw him where the lamplight fell,
Alone beside the fire;
His face was kind, his shoulders strong,
His eyes were full of tire.

And there was something in his smile
That made the whole room cease;
As though a man had wandered far
And lately come to peace.

I went to him and spoke his name,
He turned and welcomed me;
And for a little while I thought
How warm his heart might be.

He laughed, and when he laughed I saw
The boy he must have been;
And something in me leaned toward him
As branches lean to wind.

But from the corner of the room,
Where neither candle shone,
A woman stepped without a sound
And stood there all alone.

She made no threat, she spoke no word,
She did not bare her teeth;
She only wore the quiet face
Of something lost to grief.

He did not rise to welcome her,
Nor bid the shade depart;
But every now and then his eyes
Went backward from his heart.

I asked him of the mountain snow,
He answered soft and slow;
But somewhere halfway through his words
His gaze would turn and go.

I touched his hand. He touched me back.
There was no lack of flame.
And yet the woman in the dark
Still seemed to know his name.

She never crossed the room to us,
Nor laid a hand on him;
She only stood beyond the light,
Where all the walls grew dim.

And once I thought, Perhaps she leaves
If I remain till dawn.
But phantoms do not heed the clock,
Nor know when love comes on.

He looked at me as though he wished
That I would somehow stay;
And then he looked behind my chair,
And part of him went away.

Oh, I could love this man, I thought,
If he were wholly here;
I could lie down beside his life
And never live in fear.

Not fear that she would strike at me,
Or drag him through the door.
I feared the simple, quieter thing:
That he was hers before.

Or not still hers (perhaps that word
Would wrong the living man);
But grief had built her one small chair
No living hand could ban.

And every woman coming near
Could see what he could not:
There was a place beside his soul
Where someone still was not.

I wanted him. God knows I did.
I wanted all his days;
The mornings underneath his roof,
The years inside his gaze.

I wanted roads and winter fires,
His hand asleep in mine;
I wanted all the ordinary things
That make a life divine.

But every time I dreamed ahead
The phantom would appear;
Not jealous, cruel, nor beckoning.
Only unbearably near.

And so I stood and kissed his cheek
Before I turned away to go;
He asked me why. I could not say
The thing he ought to know.

How do you tell a grieving man,
Whose love is fierce and true,
“I do not fear the ghost herself,
I fear she lives with you”?

So I left him in the lamplight there,
Still beautiful, still kind;
And through the glass I saw him turn
To something far behind.

Perhaps one day another comes
And finds that corner bare;
Perhaps she takes the empty seat
And finds him wholly there.

Perhaps he hears a living voice
And does not turn away;
Perhaps the dead are finally dead,
And yesterday is day.

And if that woman loves him then,
I hope she understands:
Some men are not abandoned things.
They are returning lands.

And if she takes his offered heart
When all the ghosts are gone,
She’ll never know how many loved
The man he was becoming all along.
