title: Elegy at Horsethief
---

Beneath my boots the old roots rose  
&emsp;And twisted through the ground;  
&emsp;Then turned beneath where no wind blows,  
&emsp;Where older truths are found.

They dive beyond the silver dew,  
&emsp;Past daylight’s brittle art;  
&emsp;The matriarchs descend unseen,  
&emsp;Where silence schools the heart.

Above, the searching branches meet  
&emsp;And gather into one;  
&emsp;What wandered separately below  
&emsp;Now reaches for the sun.

The aspens spilled their yellow coins,  
&emsp;The scrub oak darkened red;  
&emsp;October hung from every limb,  
&emsp;Half glory and half dead.

For months a shade did walk with me,  
&emsp;Not separate from my mind;  
&emsp;A shape that memory gave a face  
&emsp;And would not leave behind.

It never came to sharpen fear,  
&emsp;Nor asked that I defend;  
&emsp;It wore the sorrow of the dead,  
&emsp;But only sought an end.

Along the trail once more I came,  
&emsp;Two rings within my pack;  
&emsp;The falls still called the place by name  
&emsp;And pulled the years all back.

The water fell beyond the stone,  
&emsp;The same as years before;  
&emsp;I saw the question rise again,  
&emsp;Then watched us be no more.

Beyond the falls the footpath thinned
&emsp;And yielded to the wood;
&emsp;I followed where the water went,
&emsp;As far as water would.

The stream grew still among the stones,
&emsp;Its silver nearly done;
&emsp;Beside its final shallow breath
&emsp;The branches leaned as one.

I knelt and cut the finer roots  
&emsp;And begged the trees forgive;  
&emsp;Then dug beneath their braided feet,  
&emsp;Where older answers live.

At last I held the rings once more
&emsp;Beneath the elders’ gaze;
&emsp;Two circles emptied of their hands,
&emsp;Still bright with vanished days.

I gave you all I knew to give  
&emsp;And could not longer stay;  
&emsp;I laid our circles side by side  
&emsp;And gave them earth and clay.

Sleep now, my love, beneath the loam,  
&emsp;Together, ring with ring;  
&emsp;What could not make a living home  
&emsp;May sleep through every spring.

Beyond the grave I walked alone  
&emsp;Where every footpath ceased;  
&emsp;No blazes marked the timber there,  
&emsp;No road ran west or east.

I knew a trail lay somewhere on  
&emsp;Beyond the trackless pine;  
&emsp;But for a while the path was gone,  
&emsp;And still the walk was mine.

The shape that walked beside me then  
&emsp;Grew quiet in the air;  
&emsp;I passed beneath the autumn trees,  
&emsp;And nothing followed there.
