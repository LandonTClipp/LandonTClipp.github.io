The United States has recently had a major renaissance in its understanding and acceptance of this little chemical called psilocybin. Psilocybin is the precursor of the active ingredient psilocin, which is shaped similarly to serotonin. It causes hallucinations and changes the fundamental structure of how different brain regions communicate with each other. Of course, it's colloquially known as magic mushrooms. And magic they are.

Psilocybin has been something I've been interested in for a long time. Almost serendipitously, after I moved to Colorado a few months ago, I realized that this state is only one of two (the other being Oregon) that has legalized psilocybin in therapeutic settings. Licensed clinicians supervise clients as they go through what's called the "journey." Because the effects of the chemical can be so intense and deeply moving, a lot of care must be taken in preparing properly for the journey and in the physical setting in which it takes place. Additionally, the clinicians screen for certain mental/physical disorders that would serve as contraindications to this experience. This is done to ensure you have the best possible chance at having a good journey.

The reason this chemical has been seeing increased attention over the years is that it's been shown to have clinically significant outcomes for people with certain disorders like Major Depressive Disorder, anxiety, PTSD, substance abuse, and many others. Psilocybin is currently going through a major clinical trial that is expected to convince the FDA to approve it nationwide as a legitimate form of treatment. As far as I know, I don't have any mental disorders, but regardless I felt it was going to help with some difficult life situations I was going through, as it seems to do for most people.

This post will chronicle my experience with the the 'shroom.

## Context

Going into the experience, I had spent months practicing meditation and mindfulness. I found meaning in books like The Power of Now by Eckhart Tolle, long stretches of solitude in the mountains, and the physical effort of climbing them. I was trying to understand the life I found myself in and find some peace within it. The mountains gave me enough quiet to hear what had been drowned out by the noise of ordinary life.

But that peace was difficult to carry home. I could sit beside an alpine lake and feel completely present, then find myself replaying the same painful conversations hours later. I could recognize that I was ruminating without being able to stop. Sometimes recognizing it just gave me another thing to be frustrated about. I understood the idea of letting go. Living it was harder.

That gap was part of what drew me toward the journey. In preparing for it, I thought about my relationship with control. What happened when things didn't go the way I wanted? Could I leave a painful question unanswered? Could I feel something without immediately trying to explain it, solve it, or make it disappear? These were uncomfortable questions because I could see how much energy I spent trying to think my way out of feelings.

I hoped my meditation practice would help me stay with whatever arose, even if it was confusing or painful. I had practiced observing thoughts without following them, but I didn't know how that practice would hold up during an experience I had never had. I wanted to arrive willing to encounter something I couldn't direct or immediately understand.

Looking back, there was a tension in that hope. I wanted to give up control, but I also wanted relief. Some part of me still wanted the experience to accomplish something, to quiet the thoughts I hadn't been able to quiet myself. That hope came into the room with me too.

Of the mind-altering substances I've taken, psilocybin became the most intense, the most religious, the most bizarre, and the most emotionally difficult experience I've ever had.

## Intentions

Preparing for my journey involved multiple talk therapy sessions. I was asked what my intentions going into the journey was, and I gave a nervous laugh and said that my goal is to not have any intentions. I've learned from my own mindfulness work that intentions can work against you, or at least my conception of what an "intention" was had trapped me in an egoic mind that eventually came back to destroy my sense of self. Intentions, I thought, were a form of control, and my intention was to let go of it.

Rather, I think a better question to ask is: "what are you interested in exploring?" I am _interested_ in a topic, but I will not force that topic to manifest itself. That was something I felt comfortable answering. I had been trapped in compulsive thinking about things that had happened to me, and for as much meditation I had done on the subject, I felt gripped by this compulsion. I felt moments of clarity, often when I'm at the top of the mountain beaing beaten to death by gale force winds, or knocked off my feet when nature randomly decided to reveal her celestial majesty, but what escaped me was a durable sense of stillness and peace. I knew what was happening to me, but I seemed to lack the strength to quiet that part of my mind for good. I had brief tastes of this heaven, but I still had one foot in the underworld and one in the stars.

## Pillars

The experience I had was categorized into four pillars: The Teacher, The Meditation, The Anomalies, and The Revelation. The pillars themselves shifted throughout time. For some hours, I spent everything inside of the meditation. Others, I spent in the Revelation. Some hours contained both, some contained none. And sometimes a pillar would reemerge when I thought it had left me. There was no clean temporal element to them, but each pillar was distinct and I knew when it had arrived.

### The Teacher

In the morning, I arrived at the facility where I was going to have my experience. I was sitting at an altar of different elements that my psychologist and I had put together. I brought a few meaningful pieces of artwork, poetry, artifacts of my life. Beside me was a drink I prepared that contained the ground-up mushrooms submersed in orange juice. I reached down for the cup and drank everything. It tasted kind of like an earthy milkshake. There wasn't much taste of the mushrooms themselves, but it was pleasant. I continued to sit at our altar for maybe 10 minutes and started noticing subtle visual and mental effects fairly quickly. I was trying to focus on the poem I had brought but I realized the visual elements were already too distracting to me, and I was having a hard time actually reading it. 

Sitting there, I felt as if I was suddenly being catapulted over a mountaintop. I recognized which mountain it was immediately. It was one I had spent a significant amount of time staring at when I had my [wilderness pilgrimage](2026-09-01-pilgrimage.md). It was there that I once saw a multi-colored but semi-transparent celestial being, maybe some concept of an "angel", flying over the mountaintop and looking at me from a distance. When I saw this thing, I was deeply terrified of it, but it didn't threaten me. It was almost indifferent to me, just flying over the mountains before it turned to look at me for a few brief seconds.[^1]

So I decided it was as good of a time as ever to lie down on the bed and put on the eye mask. And so it began.

A few moments after I put on the eye mask, I began seeing vague images of doors, or celestial gates, or portals that I was being asked to walk through. There wasn't much visual detail to them, it was mostly a silhouette consisting only of light and dark. I wouldn't say I walked through the portals, I almost sprinted across them because I was pretty damn excited. I'm not sure if that was the respectful thing to do. Ah, whatever.

At first, there was nothing coherent. It was not exactly chaos; it was incoherence, like sitting in an orchestra hall while all the instruments were tuning. There was sound and activity, but it had not come together into anything ordered.

The world around me shifted to the music I was listening to. The sound became extraordinarily real. During one song, nothing in particular seemed to happen beyond listening, but listening itself felt like being seated beside the musicians in a concert hall. There was a low hum that felt directional, almost as though someone had placed a speaker behind my head. I asked whether the psychologist had brought another speaker into the room. She said there was only one. The hum continued until the music changed, and then it was gone.

I saw people living on the surface of Earth's crust. This place was where all of life's problems happen. Our worries, our fears, our anxieties, death, the endings of relationships, our work stress, every negative emotion lives here. I saw that everyone was stuck on the surface of Earth's crust. Most people live their whole lives here. Then the crust cracked open. It was as if someone had driven an enormous wedge into it with a hammer and split the Earth apart. Underneath was a whole world of consciousness, beautiful and deep, extending far below those daily problems. I went into that well of light and came upon a scene of a high alpine lake. I had seen many alpine lakes, but I didn't recognize this one. There was some intelligent entity floating beside me. I asked it: "Why did you bring me here?" It turned to look at me with a smile. It said nothing, but I understood a message: "you have already seen heaven on Earth. This is your church."

### The Meditation

As the experience deepened, I felt as though the organization of my mind was dissolving. I saw flashes of fractals and twisting sprites. The order I ordinarily relied on to think and speak seemed to be coming apart, yet beneath it was a deep stillness. I sat beside an endless pool of perfectly calm water. There was nothing but this water.

Whenever I tried to put a thought into words, the attempt disturbed the stillness, as if someone had slammed a hand into the pool. I became aware of how much effort it took to say anything. I wanted to speak, but could not gather my thoughts into something I could express. Eventually, I stopped trying and allowed myself to experience what was happening.

At other moments, I deliberately tried to think about the problems in my life, curious about how they would feel in this state. Each time, I sensed an entity extending an arm to stop me. It felt as though this presence was preventing me from returning to those thoughts.

### The Anomalies

I took the eye mask off of my face and looked around the room. The ceiling was bulging and the colors were vibrant. I turned my head and looked at the picture beside me. It was a large painting of a blue flower with four petals. On the lower left petal, there was a hand reaching up to a wound where blue blood was pouring out of it. The entire picture was moving and undulating. The colors were turning in on themselves like someone kneading dough. I watched as the blood ran down the painting and to the wall. It was the strangest thing I had ever seen.

The music coming from the speaker beside me had background noises of water and tropical birds. While I was still looking at the painting, I had the distinct impression that a rainforest existed behind me, outside my field of view. I couldn't see it, but it felt as though the room extended into it.

I picked up a small slice of a tree's trunk that was the canvas for a painting of an alpine lake beneath majestic mountains. The mountains undulated like waves, and it reminded me of so many places I had been to. I recalled the San Juan Mountains, my favorite place on the whole continent. I remembered the trails I had climbed, the abandoned mines I explored, the mountains I climbed, and of course the alpine lakes I had seen. 

### The Revelation

At this point, my journey took me deep into a place that was tied with so much joy but also so much pain. This vision brought me to a place that I had lived in for a long time. I saw all the furniture, the memories, the rooms, the hallways, the decorations, the people. I kept returning to one spot in that domain: a hallway between two rooms. At this point, I was not imagining in my mind's eye. I was actually there, and I could feel and see everything.

I spent hours and hours in that hallway. I saw many visions, and realized many truths of what happened there. Some of these truths were things I had never considered before, things that in ordinary life I hadn't previously been able to access. I understood the role of what that place was to me, what I was trying to do there. I saw the limits of what I was trying to do out of love. I saw a deep tragedy, things I could not stop from happening, a suffering I could not prevent. There was deep sadness and grief. I saw the futility of what I tried to do, the failure of the protection I tried to give. The battle I waged was pointed in the wrong direction. The battle was actually never mine to wage in the first place.

At one point, I began rising into the sky away from that place, and behind me everything began to disintegrate into the air like ash rising from a fire. I turned around and what remained was a meadow full of flowers, grass, and a warm wind in my face. There was new growth forming around what was left behind. The outline of the past was there in the ground, but the contours of those things had been softened by the passage of time.

An entire novel could be written about what I experienced in that place, but it shall be a book I will write only for myself.

## Integration

I'm writing this report only two days after my journey. These last two days have been extremely tiring. The day after my journey, I felt like I had a constant low-grade headache. This feeling likely came from the fact that I had done so much intense mental and emotional processing. I'm sure you've had a similar experience after trying to understand a really complex concept, like something with math or computers.

The period of integration is going to last a few weeks ahead of me, but I've already noticed some subtle changes that I'm sure will morph into something more robust and lasting. The compulsive thinking has quieted. I'm able to go longer stretches of uninterrupted stillness, even when doing something that requires a lot of attention like driving. My mind seems to simply experience things rather than ruminate.

More than anything, this journey was one of the most intense emotional experiences of my life. It also was not at all what I expected from the drug, and there's really no way to accurately explain what it feels like. Words simply cannot convey the meaning I received or how wildly different the architecture of your brain feels while in the throes of the experience. 

[^1]: Oh yeah, I should mention that I was under the influence of a shit ton of weed. That might have something to do with this being.
