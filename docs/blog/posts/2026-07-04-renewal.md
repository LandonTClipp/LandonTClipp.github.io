title: Beyond the Burn

<div class="poem" markdown>

<div class="poem-text">

The forest keeps its patient tongue,  
Though fire has stripped the pine.  
Tanagers still greet the dawn,  
Though they were never mine.

I sit beside an empty page,  
As still as frozen streams.  
The words I carried through the dark  
Now hide beyond my dreams.

The blackened trunks still mark the hill,  
Where summer shadows played.  
The wind recalls forgotten branch,  
Yet none can grant their shade.

The forest that I'd grown to love  
Was never mine to keep.  
The fire carried all away,  
And left the roots to sleep.

No branch can wear its needles twice,  
Nor ash become the tree.  
The hills remember what they were,  
Though changed they'll always be.

Yet deep beneath the charcoal earth,  
Where no eye ever turns,  
The hidden roots still gather rain  
Long after cedar burns.

The cones unseal their guarded seed,  
Awakened by the flame.  
The soil, enriched by what was lost,  
Will never grow the same.

First comes the moss upon the stone,  
Then fireweed's purple bloom.  
The aspen lifts a trembling leaf  
Where once there stood but gloom.

The forest does not seek its past,  
Nor beg the flames to cease.  
It gathers what the seasons give  
And fashions them to peace.
